## Section 1: Visual Identity Statement

> The right visual choice is always the one where the environment feels like it's actively trying to survive, transform, or consume the player — making the world itself feel like the antagonist.

### Supporting Principles

**Principle 1: Environmental Transformation**
- Definition: Every space — from the mountain prison to infected towns — shows visible signs of the alien infection's spread, with biological wrongness overtaking human infrastructure.
- Design test: When deciding between a clean environment and one showing organic corruption, this principle says choose the transformation — even in early-game "safe" areas, hint at what's coming.

**Principle 2: Earned Revelation Through Scarcity**
- Definition: Discoverable clues, survivor notes, and hidden locations are visually understated, rewarding careful observation rather than demanding attention.
- Design test: When a critical lore detail competes with visual noise (signage, props, lighting), this principle says reduce visual density — make players earn their discoveries through attention, not spectacle.

**Principle 3: Survival Tension Through Visual Legibility**
- Definition: The player character's limited resources (ammo, medical supplies, food) are visually distinct and scarce, creating constant visual reminder of vulnerability.
- Design test: When choosing between making resources blend in versus standing out as precious, this principle says make scarcity obvious — seeing "only two bullets left" should be legible in a tense moment.

## Section 3: Shape Language

### 1. Character Silhouette Philosophy

**Silhouette doctrine**: Every character archetype must be readable at 64×64 pixels. Silhouettes must work against ANY background — infected town, alien hive, or prison wall. If the shape only reads on "clean" backgrounds, it's too weak.

**Silhouette isolation test**: Friend/foe/neutral communicated through shape language alone:

| Archetype | Shape Language | Primary silhouette | Readability anchor |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Player** | Wide shoulders, vertical head space, center-weighted mass, arms slightly away (ready stance) | Vertical dominant | Tallest point = head; "standing tall" vs. world leaning in |
| **Alien-infected** | Compressed vertical, sprawling limbs, asymmetric mass, head sinks into shoulder | Horizontal dominant | Organic wrongness — shape feels "attempted," not designed |
| **Survivor NPC** | Compact, bundled mass, protected torso, items held close | Compressed vertical | Hunching — "this person is hiding/surviving" |
| **Boss entity** | Massive footprint, dominant vertical, then shifts to alien geometry | Scale shift | Boss BREAKS silhouette rules; where everything compresses, boss expands |

**Friend/Foe/Neutral at a glance**:
- **Friend**: Vertical axis dominant, arms at sides or holding close — "contained" shape language
- **Foe**: Horizontal axis dominant, limbs extending outward, asymmetry — "reaching" shape language
- **Neutral**: Compressed vertical, bundled, no extension — "hidden" shape language

### 2. Environment Geometry

**Dominant geometry**: Angular (human-made) progressively contaminated by organic wrongness.

| Zone type | Primary geometry | Contamination markers | Emotional message |
|----------|---------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Mountain prison** | Heavy blocks, grid windows, brutalist concrete | Sharp angles dominate; even damage is angular | "Impenetrable but not safe" |
| **Infected towns** | Organic spread overriding right angles | Curves breach straight lines; walls bulge outward | "Life finds a way, wrong" |
| **Alien hives** | Non-Euclidean volumes, gravity has opinions | Vertical leans; openings have no reference | Reality is optional here |

**Architectural grammar per zone**:
- **Prison**: Weight goes down, not up. Windows tall and narrow. Corridors compress then expand. Every corner has sight lines.
- **Infected towns**: Grammar degrades — right angles become 80° or 100°. Walls bulge. Vines grow ALONG walls, not through them (infection learning human geometry).
- **Alien hives**: Grammar breaks entirely. Ceilings optional. Openings are smooth transitions. Nothing casts a shadow that makes sense.

**Shape language progression as infection spreads**:
```
Stage 0 (0–20%): Angles hold. Building is wounded but itself.
Stage 1 (20–50%): Angles soften. Curves appear at seam lines.
Stage 2 (50–80%): Majority organic. Angular remnants stand like teeth in a mouth.
Stage 3 (80–100%): All structure is framework for growth. Building IS the infection's skeleton.
```

**Boss entity silhouette**: Hybrid progression — early phase = infected human deformation (biological horror). Late phase = alien intelligence using human material (constructed geometry that's wrong).

**Safe zone signature**: Each safe zone has different original architecture — prison zone safe space vs. town vs. camp each retain their origin geometry, unified only by being uninfected.

### 3. UI Shape Grammar

**The HUD is a rationing interface** — designed for a well-resourced world and stripped down to survive.

| HUD element | Shape language | What it communicates |
|------------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Health bar** | Fragmented rectangle, not full bar | "You're not whole; you never were" |
| **Ammo counter** | Single-digit numbers, no fractions | Count them — you know the exact count |
| **Stamina meter** | Compressed line, full length is rare | Always depleting, rarely full |
| **Threat indicator** | Angular when clear, fractures when threatened | Clarity degrades under stress |

**Shape degradation under scarcity**:
- Resources full → clean rectangles, sharp corners, contained shapes
- Resources low → shapes "melt" — corners round, edges become organic, the UI looks like it's being consumed
- Scarcity communicated through shape WITHOUT reading numbers

### 4. Hero Shapes vs. Supporting Shapes

**Hero props (discovery shape language)**: Objects that matter have unexpected geometry in context. In a world of broken horizontal lines, anything vertical draws the eye. In a landscape of leaning walls, anything upright is magnetic.

| Element type | Hero treatment | Supporting treatment |
|-------------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Key discoveries** | Vertical orientation in leaning world | Horizontal — recedes except as contrast |
| **Path markers** | Geometry doesn't match zone | Matches zone grammar — camouflages |
| **Safe zones** | Rectangles not consumed | Organic contamination at edges |
| **Threats** | Asymmetry, extension, reaching | Chaos — all background |

**Navigation without waypoints**:
1. **Clean geometry = path**: Infection hasn't reached = passable
2. **Vertical = objective**: Vertical survivors stand out as points of interest
3. **Light falls wrong = danger**: Shadows that don't match light source indicate alien presence

### 5. Alien Infection Shape Language

**The infection has its own shape grammar — distinct from human architecture.**

| Element | Human architecture | Alien infection | Emotional message |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Growth direction** | Up and out | Inward and through | It's not building — it's filling |
| **Surface quality** | Measurable, right angles | Near-angles off by 5–15° | Infection approximates human geometry but doesn't care |
| **Connection points** | Joints, intersections | Tendrils meeting at mounded nodes | It's a network, not a structure — it's alive |
| **Scale** | Human-scaled | Body-scale to environment-scale | It's growing, depending on need |

**Organic wrongness vs. structured wrongness**:
- **Organic wrongness**: The alien growth is baseline wrong — tendrils, nodes, fleshy geometry
- **Structured wrongness**: Human geometry WRONG — walls at wrong angles, floors that slope. This is where the horror lives.
- **The combination is the horror**: Organic = alien presence. Structured wrongness = it's learning our world, breaking our rules.

## Section 4: Color System

### Design Foundation

Every color is a **communication signal**, not decoration. Colors are defined by *role*, not hex value — implementation varies by zone and lighting state, but meaning is fixed.

### 4.1 Primary Palette

| Name | Role | What It Means | Implementation |
|------|------|--------------|----------------|
| **Prison Red** | Human threat | Guards, emergency strobes, searchlights — early-game terror trains fear of red, real threat is green | Cool blue-red undertone. Flickering/strobing. Never ambient. |
| **Alien Verdant** | Alien presence | Infection IS this color. Every alien growth, spore cloud, bio-mass shares this signature. Green = run. | Pulses when alive. Intensity tracks proximity. |
| **Survivor Ember** | Human safety | Camp fires, flashlight glow, friendly NPC lanterns. The only warm color in a hostile world | Soft falloff, low intensity, never dominates. |
| **Sky Veil** | Atmosphere/distance | Sky, twilight, ambient fog — cold indifference beyond human scale | Always cool. Pushed toward violet in Open World. |
| **Bioluminescent Core** | Alien life | Aliens emit light to hunt, communicate, dominate territory. Cyan-magenta range | Organic pulse rhythm, source-lit on creature geometry. |
| **Bone White** | Clarity/moment of truth | Muzzle flash, investigation light — NOW YOU SEE IT | Short-lived, high intensity, casts shadows. |
| **Shadow Charcoal** | Void/infection dominance | Infection has consumed everything — walls, floors, ceilings coated in organic matter | Becomes background as infection spreads. |

### 4.2 Semantic Color Usage

| Color | Meaning in This World | Rule |
|-------|----------------------|------|
| **Red** | Human danger (prison/guards/alarms) | Infection is green — early-game reflex weaponized |
| **Green** | Alien infection / organic wrongness | No organic green = safety anywhere |
| **Amber/Orange** | Human survival / hope | Emotional anchor — warmth is precious |
| **Blue/Violet** | Cold / isolation / human enemy equipment | Not safe — sky, night, human guards in open world |
| **White** | Player agency / clarity | Your muzzle flash, your flashlight, your discovery |
| **Cyan/Magenta** | Alien bioluminescence / boss threat | Beautiful + wrong — awe and dread simultaneously |

### 4.3 Per-Zone Color Temperature

| Zone | Primary | Rule |
|-----|---------|------|
| **Prison Escape** | Deep cool blue moonlight | Restrict to blue/gray/charcoal + red only. Color-starved. |
| **Open World** | Warm amber → cool violet transition | Amber-to-violet transition is mandatory. Full palette range. |
| **Infected Zone** | Sickly green-yellow | Palette contracts as infection deepens. Human color stripped. |
| **Survivor Camp** | Warm firelight orange/yellow | No green, no red, no bioluminescence inside camp. Fire only. |
| **Combat** | Flash-lit (white muzzle, orange explosion) | White/orange = player colors. Enemy = green/red. |
| **Boss Confrontation** | Near-total darkness | Boss bioluminescence is only light. Player flashlight is precious. |

### 4.4 UI Palette

The HUD **diverges** from the world palette — tactical overlay, not immersive.

| Semantic role | World Color | UI Color | Rationale |
|-------------|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Danger | Prison Red | Deep red (#8B1E1E) | UI red is desaturated, readable without flashing |
| Infection | Alien Verdant | Toxic green (#5C8A3C) | UI green is legible, distinct from world green |
| Safety/Human | Survivor Ember | Amber (#C9A227) | Warm but not bright — usable in all conditions |
| Alien/Elite | Bioluminescent | Cyan (#2DD4BF) | UI cyan is readable |
| Player agency | Bone White | Off-white (#E8E4DC) | Warm off-white avoids harshness |
| Void | Shadow Charcoal | UI charcoal (#1A1A1F) | UI black has higher contrast |

### 4.5 Colorblind Safety

**Critical risk: Red–Green confusion (deuteranopia/protanopia)** — approximately 8% of male players.

| Element | Color signal | Backup (REQUIRED) |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Infection zones | Green | Organic pulsing silhouette + bio-drone audio + controller vibration |
| Guard searchlights | Red strobe | Sweeping motion animation + siren audio pattern |
| Health critical | Red | Shape (segment depletion) + icon (heart/cross) |
| Infection meter | Green fill | Shape (border animation) + biohazard icon |
| Safe zone | Amber | Campfire icon silhouette + text label |
| Loot | Amber sparkle | Icon + item silhouette + sound |
| Enemy detection | Red pulse | Alert triangle shape + audio cue |

**General rule**: No semantic information is communicated by color alone. Every color signal has at least one additional channel: shape, animation, audio, or text.

## Section 5: Character Design Direction

### 5.1 Player Character Visual Archetype

**Archetype**: Black ops mercenary — tactical, practical, human. Every piece of gear serves a function. No cosmetic flourishes.

**Reference**: The Last of Us Part II (Ellie/Joel grounded practicality), Dying Light (parkour survivor gear), The Division (tactical vest loadout).

- **Silhouette**: Vertical bias — broad shoulders, narrow hips, wide stance. Creates inherent stability and capability.
- **Gear loadout**: Visible sidearm, chest rig, purpose-built boots. Clothing layers for climate adaptability.
- **Conditioning**: Gear shows wear proportional to playtime — scuffs, fade, dirt accumulation. Survival time is visible.

### 5.2 Distinguishing Features Per Character Type

| Character Type | Visual Key | Read Distance |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Player** | Tactical vest + visible weapon + grounded stance | T-shaped silhouette at thumbnail |
| **Infected Human** | Organic growth breaking through skin + asymmetric distortion | Silhouette disruption — limbs at wrong angles |
| **Survivor NPC** | Faction-coded colors + layered clothing + gear condition | Color pop + human proportions |
| **Boss Entity** | Hybrid progression + scale shift from Stage 4 infection | Mass imbalance — too large for environment |

**Rule**: No two character types share silhouette vocabulary. Player and Survivor share "human" proportions only; faction colors differentiate.

### 5.3 Expression & Pose Style Targets

**Style**: Grounded and reactive. Emotional performance through body language, not facial animation dependency.

- **Emotional range**: Conducted through pose — crouching for stealth, weapon up for alert, limping for injury
- **Reactive baseline**: Characters always responding to something. Idle is never truly idle — scanning, listening, bracing
- **Avoid**: Heroic power poses, dramatic flourishes, "I am the hero" framing
- **Tone**: Someone who has killed before and doesn't enjoy it

### 5.4 LOD Philosophy

Silhouette preserved at game distance. Player must identify "human ally" vs. "infected threat" vs. "survivor NPC" at maximum draw distance.

| Distance | Level | What Reads |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| Near (0–20m) | Full | Facial features, gear detail, wounds |
| Mid (20–50m) | Silhouette | Weapon + faction color + infection stage |
| Far (50m+) | Silhouette | Primary mass — human vs. infected vs. boss |
| Thumbnail | Silhouette | One secondary feature — infection lumps or faction color |

### 5.5 Infected Transformation Stages

| Stage | Visual Description | Read Key |
|-------|-----------------|---------|
| **Stage 0 (Freshly Infected)** | Human baseline. Early nodules on neck/jaw. Behavioral change subtle. | Nodules — small bumps under skin on neck |
| **Stage 1 (Early Mutation)** | Nodules break skin surface. Asymmetric growth on one side. Movement organic/jerky. | Skin break + asymmetry |
| **Stage 2 (Established)** | Growths multiply. Limbs elongate. Eyes clouded or missing. Secondary growths on torso. | Multiple growths + distorted proportions |
| **Stage 3 (Advanced Hybrid)** | Human shape eroding. Major limbs merge or duplicate. Chitin underlayer exposed. | Human outline dissolving |
| **Stage 4 (Full Transformation)** | Fully alien form. No human reference points. Non-Euclidean mass. | Complete unreadability = maximum threat |

**Critical rule**: A player must identify infection stage at a glance. Stage 0 reads as "something wrong." Stage 4 reads as "run."

## Section 6: Environment Design Language

### 6.1 Architectural Style

Three distinct vocabularies, each tied to a zone type and pillar.

**A. Brutalist Mountain Prison**
- **Materials**: Cast concrete, reinforced steel, industrial grating. Oppressive weight.
- **Grid**: Rigid 90-degree geometry. Corridors funnel. Guard towers with sightline priority.
- **Emotional tone**: Cold, institutional, indifferent to human comfort. Failed at its job.
- **Pillar alignment**: Serves "hostile environment as antagonist" — architecture works against the player.

**B. Infected Towns**
- **Materials**: Human decay (peeling paint, broken glass, rusted metal) + organic contamination.
- **Grid**: Organic deviation. Roads cracked. Buildings listing. Growths forming wrong structures.
- **Emotional tone**: Violated domesticity. These were homes. Now incubators.
- **Pillar alignment**: Serves "hope at tremendous cost" — evidence of human life makes the theft visible.

**C. Alien Hives**
- **Materials**: Living membrane, chitin surfaces, bioluminescent nodes, impossible geometry.
- **Grid**: Non-Euclidean. Corridors loop. Stairs go up into the ceiling. Anti-architecture.
- **Emotional tone**: Cosmic wrongness. The infection's actual home. Alien logic.
- **Pillar alignment**: Serves "world actively transforming" — the world is consuming, not just present.

### 6.2 Texture Philosophy

**Engine target**: Photorealistic PBR with Substrate materials (UE 5.7).

| Zone | Primary Material | Surface Treatment |
|-----|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Prison** | Matte concrete, industrial steel | Dust, water staining, chip/impact damage |
| **Infected Town** | Human material (peeling/broken) + organic (wet growth, membrane) | Organic progressively dominates |
| **Alien Hive** | Living chitin, membrane, bioluminescent bloom | Self-maintaining — no damage, just growth |

**Substrate rule**: Infection surfaces always wet/specular. Light reflecting wrong IS how players identify infection at distance without seeing growths.

### 6.3 Prop Density Rules

| Zone | Density Target | Driver |
|-----|---------------|--------|
| **Prison** | Sparse to mid | Military precision. Scavenging reveals what's missing. |
| **Infected Town** | Mid to high (progressive) | Organic overgrowth competes with human objects. Density = infection timeline. |
| **Alien Hive** | High (saturated) | Biological abundance — not clutter, growth. Everything alive. |

**Progressive density rule**: Within infected town zones, density increases toward hive centers.

### 6.4 Environmental Storytelling Guidelines

**Principle**: Every visual detail carries narrative without text.

**What to show**:
- Infection timeline: Wetness, growth thickness, membrane color (pale = early, bioluminescent = late)
- Human behavior at interruption: Half-eaten meals, barricaded doors, a single shoe
- Survivor faction origins: Faction colors on walls, barricade style, abandoned equipment
- Failure of containment: Breach damage type (force from inside vs. outside)

**What to avoid**:
- Scavenger pickups without environmental sense
- Environmental narrative contradicting gameplay
- Clean spaces in infected zones (absence of contamination must be plot-relevant, not oversight)

## Section 7: UI/HUD Visual Direction

### 7.1 Visual Style

**Philosophy**: Immersive-first by default. Screen-space HUD hidden during normal play — information delivered through world props, character animation, and reactive elements. A full tactical HUD is available as a **player-configurable toggle** in settings.

**Default mode (Immersive)**:
- Health communicated through player animation (limping, stagger) + vignette pulse when critical
- Ammo shown on weapon model + audio callout ("two rounds remaining")
- Threat detection through world elements (searchlights, alert icons on world objects) + audio cues
- Investigation prompts as world overlays — context-sensitive, not persistent
- Loot identified by subtle glow outline (player-configurable: on/off)

**Toggle mode (Full HUD)**:
- Standard tactical overlay — health bar, ammo counter, threat indicator, objective marker
- Fully functional for accessibility or preference

### 7.2 Configurability Layer

Full settings menu with options:
- **HUD visibility**: Immersive (default) / Minimal / Full
- **Damage indicators**: Off / Subtle (vignette) / Full
- **Loot highlighting**: Off / On
- **Threat markers**: Off / On
- **Tutorial prompts**: Off / On
- **Audio callouts**: Off / On (ammo count spoken aloud)

### 7.3 Typography

| Hierarchy | Use case | Sizing principle |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|
| **Critical** | Health, ammo, active threat | Large (48–64pt), top-aligned |
| **Primary** | Inventory, objective | Medium (24–32pt), mid-screen |
| **Secondary** | Flavor text, timestamps | Small (14–18pt), low opacity |

**Font criteria**: Monospace or condensed sans-serif for numeric data. No ambiguous characters (0/O, 1/l/I). No decorative fonts.

### 7.4 Iconography

Outlined only. 2px stroke minimum. Readable at 32×32px. Text label on first encounter, icon-only after.

### 7.5 Animation

| Type | Duration | Curve |
|------|----------|-------|
| Element appear | 100–150ms | Ease-out |
| Element disappear | 80–100ms | Ease-in |
| State change | 150ms | Linear |
| Degradation (scarce resources) | Shapes "melt" — corners round, edges organic | Continuous |

### 7.6 Input Alignment

Full keyboard + gamepad parity. No hover-only interactions. Clear gamepad focus indicator.

## Section 8: Asset Standards

### 8.1 File Formats

| Type | Preferred | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Meshes** | FBX (binary) | No Collada or OBJ for production |
| **Textures** | PNG / TGA | **JPG banned** — lossy compression accumulates |
| **Materials** | Substrate (UE 5.7) | Standard for modern workflows |
| **Audio** | WAV (source) / OGG (runtime) | MP3 acceptable if bandwidth-critical |

### 8.2 Naming Convention

**Format**: `[Zone]_[Type]_[Name]_[LOD#]`

**Example**: `PRISON_PROP_CRATE_A_LOD0`

**Forbidden**: Generic names, date stamps, personal identifiers.

### 8.3 Texture Resolution Tiers

| Tier | Use | Resolution |
|------|-----|-----------|
| **2K Hero** | Player character, hero props, bosses | 2048×2048 |
| **1K–2K Standard** | Environment props, architecture | 1024–2048 |
| **2K–4K Surface** | Large surfaces (Nanite meshes) | 2048–4096 |
| **512–1K UI** | HUD, icons | 512–1024 |

All textures: powers of two, full mipmap chains, no pre-multiplied alpha.

### 8.4 Poly Count Budgets

| Asset | Triangle Budget |
|-------|--------------|
| **Player character** | 100,000–150,000 tris |
| **Major NPCs** | 50,000–100,000 tris |
| **Infected enemies** | 30,000–80,000 tris |
| **Boss entities** | 150,000–300,000 tris |
| **Hero props** | 5,000–20,000 tris |
| **Environment props** | 1,000–10,000 tris |
| **Large environment** | Nanite (virtualized) |

### 8.5 Material Slot Counts

| Asset | Max Materials | Note |
|------|-------------|------|
| **Standard static meshes** | 4 | Substrate layering enables complexity |
| **Player character** | 6–8 | Head, body, armor, weapon, effects |
| **Enemies** | 4–6 | Simplified from player |

### 8.6 Performance Budget

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| **Framerate** | 60fps |
| **Frame budget** | 16.6ms |
| **Draw calls** | <2000 per frame |
| **Texture memory** | <512MB resident |
| **Shadow render** | <2ms |
| **Nanite raster** | <3ms |
| **Lumen GI** | <4ms |
| **Post-processing** | <1.5ms |

## Section 7: UI/HUD Visual Direction

### Overview

The HUD is a **tactical overlay — a tool, not an immersive element**. Screen-space UI is minimal and functional, serving survival rather than world-building. Diegetic elements (flashlights, survivor notes, world maps) exist as in-world props. The UI's visual language reinforces that the player is rationing, scrounging, and surviving — the interface itself is a product of scarcity.

---

### 7.1 Visual Style

**Philosophy**: Functional minimalism. Every element earns its screen space.

| Element type | Treatment | Rationale |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| Screen-space HUD | Flat 2D overlay, no faux-3D depth | Readable at a glance during combat |
| Critical info | Large, top-aligned, always visible | Player never obscures essential data |
| Secondary info | Smaller, peripheral positioning | Available but not distracting |
| Diegetic UI | Physical props (notes, maps, PDA screens) | Player discovers information organically |

**Tension point**: Immersive UI styles (subtle, translucent, peripheral) risk readability in high-tension moments. The design decision favors **legibility over immersion** — the world is hostile; the HUD doesn't need to be atmospheric.

**No hover-only interactions**: Every element works with keyboard navigation and gamepad input (d-pad focus, thumbstick selection). No element requires mouse hover to reveal information.

---

### 7.2 Typography

**Reference**: TLOU2's utilitarian military typography — clean, readable, no decorative flourishes.

| Hierarchy | Use case | Sizing principle |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| **Critical** | Health, ammo count, active threat | Large (48–64pt equivalent), top-aligned, high contrast |
| **Primary** | Inventory slots, objective markers | Medium (24–32pt equivalent), mid-screen |
| **Secondary** | Subtle hints, flavor text, timestamps | Small (14–18pt equivalent), low opacity |

**Font selection criteria**:
- Monospace or condensed sans-serif for numeric data (ammo, health values)
- Clear letterform distinction at small sizes (no ambiguous characters: 0/O, 1/l/I)
- No script, decorative, or highly-stylized fonts

**Color in typography**: Type color communicates semantic state (red = danger, green = infection, amber = human safety). Size and position communicate priority. Color is never the only signal — see Section 4.5 for colorblind safety requirements.

---

### 7.3 Iconography

**Style**: Outlined, high-contrast, readable at 32×32 pixels.

| Standard | Specification |
|----------|---------------|
| **Fill style** | Outlined only — no filled icons |
| **Stroke weight** | 2px minimum for legibility |
| **Contrast** | White stroke on dark backing, or dark stroke on light |
| **Size minimum** | 32×32px per icon |
| **Text pairing** | Every icon has a text label on first encounter |

**Icon set design principles**:
1. **Silhouette-first**: Icon must communicate meaning through shape, not detail
2. **Scalability**: Test at 32×32, 48×48, and 64×64 for different HUD densities
3. **Consistency**: All icons from same stroke family, same optical weight
4. **Semantic alignment**: Icons match color system (Section 4.4 UI Palette)

**First-encounter labeling**: When a new icon appears, display text label for 5 seconds or until acknowledged. After that, icon-only display is acceptable.

---

### 7.4 Animation

**Core principle**: Transitions are fast and functional — no bounce, no elastic, no overshoot.

| Animation type | Duration | Curve | Rationale |
|---------------|----------|-------|-----------|
| Element appear | 100–150ms | Ease-out | Quick, no drama |
| Element disappear | 80–100ms | Ease-in | Faster exit than enter |
| State change (ammo -1) | 150ms | Linear | Immediate feedback |
| Screen transition | 200–250ms | Ease-in-out | Deliberate, not playful |

**Degradation animations** — the HUD shows the world consuming it:
- Health drops to critical → UI shapes "melt" (corners round, edges become organic, shape integrity fails)
- Ammo depletes → counter segments fragment and drift
- Stamina exhausted → stamina bar dissolves from ends inward
- Scarcity communicated through shape **without requiring number reading**

**Performance note**: UI animations are GPU-accelerated (UE5 UMG materials) where possible to avoid frame budget impact.

---

### 7.5 Input Alignment

**Supported input methods**:
- **Keyboard**: Full navigation, rebindable keys
- **Gamepad**: D-pad navigation, thumbstick selection, face buttons for actions
- **Mouse**: Point-and-click as optional enhancement (not required)

**No exclusive interactions**:
- Hover-only tooltips → must have keyboard/gamepad equivalent
- Drag-and-drop without keyboard alternative → must have confirm/cancel button pair
- Scroll-wheel-only content → must have scrollbar + arrow key navigation

**Gamepad focus model**:
- Clear focus indicator (high-contrast ring, not subtle glow)
- 8-way directional focus navigation
- Wrap-around disabled (intentional — prevents disorientation)

**Accessibility floor**: All HUD elements meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast ratios (4.5:1 minimum for text, 3:1 for UI components).

## Section 8: Asset Standards

### Overview

This section defines the production pipeline from source assets to runtime performance. Standards ensure consistent quality, manageable file sizes, and predictable frame budgets. All decisions trace to the performance target: **60fps, 16.6ms frame budget, <2000 draw calls per frame at high settings.**

---

### 8.1 File Format Preferences

| Asset type | Preferred format | Rejection reason for alternatives |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| **Meshes** | FBX (binary) | Collada lacks feature parity; OBJ lacks materials |
| **Textures** | PNG (lossless) / TGA (uncompressed) | **JPG is banned for game assets** — lossy compression accumulates across pipeline, causing texture degradation |
| **Materials** | Substrate (Unreal 5) | Standard for modern Unreal workflows |
| **Audio** | WAV (source) / OGG (runtime) | MP3 acceptable for runtime if bandwidth is critical |

**Pipeline rule**: Source files (PSD, MA, ZPR) retained in version control. Intermediate files (FBX, PNG/TGA) regenerated from source as needed.

**No JPG policy**: Any texture pipeline that results in JPG-compressed output is rejected. This includes:
- Export settings marked as "JPEG" or "compressed"
- Third-party tools that auto-compress
- Store formats that imply quality loss

---

### 8.2 Naming Convention

**Standard format**: `[Zone]_[Type]_[Name]_[LOD#]`

**Pattern**: `PRISON_PROP_CRATE_A_LOD0`

| Component | Definition | Example |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Zone** | Game zone identifier (3-8 chars) | PRISON, TOWN_01, HIVE_ALPHA |
| **Type** | Asset category | PROP, ARCH, CHAR, ENEMY, VFX |
| **Name** | Descriptive unique name | CRATE_A, WALL_CORNER, NPC_SURVIVOR_01 |
| **LOD** | Level of detail | LOD0, LOD1, LOD2, LOD3 |

**Additional qualifiers** (when needed): `_DAMAGED`, `_VARIANT_B`, `_ANIMATED`

**Scanner test**: A new team member should be able to identify the asset's zone, type, and approximate location in the hierarchy from the name alone.

**Forbidden**: Generic names (`prop_001`, `new_material`), date stamps (`v2_final_REAL`), or personal identifiers (`jsmith_custom`).

---

### 8.3 Texture Resolution Tiers

| Tier | Use case | Resolution | Anisotropic filtering |
|------|---------|-----------|----------------------|
| **2K Hero** | Player character faces, hero props, boss entities | 2048×2048 | Required (16x) |
| **1K–2K Standard** | Environment props, architectural trim | 1024–2048 | Required (8x) |
| **2K–4K Surface** | Large floor/wall surfaces (Nanite meshes) | 2048–4096 | Variable (matches Nanite LOD) |
| **512–1K UI** | HUD textures, icons, interface elements | 512–1024 | N/A (2D assets) |

**Mipmap policy**: All textures generate full mipmap chains. No "mipmap off" without explicit technical approval.

**Power-of-two rule**: All textures are powers of two (512, 1024, 2048, 4096). Non-power-of-two causes texture sampling artifacts.

**Tension point**: Nanite handles LOD automatically for large meshes, but texture resolution still matters — 4K surfaces with 4K textures at distance can exceed texture budget. Use 2K textures on Nanite surfaces unless close-up viewing is expected.

---

### 8.4 LOD Level Expectations

| Asset class | LOD levels | LOD distance thresholds (approx.) |
|------------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| **Player character** | 4 (LOD0–3) | LOD0: 0–5m, LOD1: 5–15m, LOD2: 15–40m, LOD3: 40m+ |
| **Major NPCs** | 4 (LOD0–3) | LOD0: 0–3m, LOD1: 3–10m, LOD2: 10–25m, LOD3: 25m+ |
| **Infected enemies** | 3 (LOD0–2) | LOD0: 0–3m, LOD1: 3–15m, LOD2: 15m+ |
| **Boss entities** | 4 (LOD0–3) | LOD0: 0–10m, LOD1: 10–30m, LOD2: 30–60m, LOD3: 60m+ |
| **Environment props** | 3 (LOD0–2) | LOD0: 0–5m, LOD1: 5–20m, LOD2: 20m+ |
| **Large environment** | Nanite (automatic) | No manual LOD — virtualized geometry |

**LOD transition smoothness**: Use HDAs or per-mesh Screen Size threshold to avoid popping. Target <5% screen size jump between LODs.

---

### 8.5 Poly Count Budgets

| Asset class | Triangle budget | Notes |
|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Player character** | 100,000–150,000 tris | Includes all clothing, accessories, weapons |
| **Major NPCs** | 50,000–100,000 tris | Faction leaders, quest-critical characters |
| **Infected enemies** | 30,000–80,000 tris | Organic deformation is texture-driven, not poly-driven |
| **Boss entities** | 150,000–300,000 tris | High-priority targets; extra budget justified |
| **Hero props** | 5,000–20,000 tris | Key story objects, interactable items |
| **Environment props** | 1,000–10,000 tris | Standard props, clutter, set dressing |
| **Large environment** | Nanite (virtualized) | No manual poly count — Unreal handles density |

**Draw call optimization**: Combine static meshes where possible. Props sharing materials should share meshes. Use instancing for repeated elements (crates, chairs, debris).

**Tension point**: Infected enemies are organic, so high-poly doesn't always mean "more detailed" — the horror comes from shader effects (parallax, displacement, subsurface), not geometry count. Organic enemies hit the lower end of the budget; human enemies (prison guards) hit the upper end.

---

### 8.6 Material Slot Counts

| Asset class | Max materials | Rationale |
|-------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Static meshes (standard)** | 4 materials max | Substrate layering enables complex surfaces within budget |
| **Static meshes (hero props)** | 6 materials max | Key interactables get extra budget |
| **Player character** | 6–8 materials | Head, body, armor pieces, weapon attachment points, effects |
| **Major NPCs** | 5–7 materials | Slightly reduced from player |
| **Skeletal meshes (enemies)** | 4–6 materials | Simplified from player |

**Substrate advantage**: Substrate materials can layer multiple surfaces (metal, rust, grime, moss) into a single material slot. Use Substrate to maximize surface variety without increasing slot count.

**Forbidden**: More than stated materials without technical approval. Material count is not flexible — it directly affects draw calls.

---

### 8.7 Importer Constraints

| Setting | Specification | Applies to |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| **Tangents** | Generate MikkTSpace tangents on import | All meshes |
| **Normals** | Recompute normals on import | Organic meshes (infected, creatures) |
| **Normals** | Use source normals | Hard-surface meshes (architecture, props) |
| **Alpha** | No pre-multiplied alpha | All transparent materials |
| **Scale** | Verify import scale (cm = 1 unit) | All meshes |

**Import validation checklist**:
1. Check for missing normals (import as "Compute")
2. Verify tangent basis (MikkTSpace required)
3. Confirm material slot count against budget
4. Validate LOD chain presence (LOD0 minimum required)

**Automated validation**: QA script runs on every import to flag budget overages before asset enters the pipeline.

---

### 8.8 Performance Budget

**Frame budget**: 16.6ms per frame (60fps target)

| Subsystem | Budget allocation | Notes |
|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| **Draw calls** | <2000 per frame (high settings) | Combine meshes, use instancing |
| **Texture streaming** | <512MB resident texture memory | Prioritize hero assets |
| **Shadow maps** | <2ms shadow render time | Cascade shadows: 3 cascades max |
| **Nanite** | <3ms nanite rasterization | Virtualized geometry handles complexity |
| **Lumen** | <4ms global illumination | Reduce GI cache density in dense areas |
| **Post-processing** | <1.5ms frame buffer processing | Limit stacked effects |

**Monitoring**:
- Use UE5's Session Frontend or Pixel Inspector for per-frame breakdown
- GPU dump analysis for shader complexity spikes
- Target: No single frame exceeds 14ms GPU time (leaving 2ms headroom)

**Tension points**:
- **Nanite vs. Draw Calls**: Nanite reduces poly count concerns but increases GPU rasterization. Balance Nanite density with overall scene complexity.
- **Texture resolution vs. Streaming**: Higher-res textures look better but stress memory. 2K hero assets are non-negotiable; 1K surfaces are acceptable.
- **Lumen GI vs. Frame Budget**: Lumen's quality settings are frame-budget-sensitive. Lock GI settings per quality tier.

## Section 9: Reference Direction

| Reference | Draw From | Avoid / Diverge | Why It Matters |
|-----------|----------|----------------|---------------|
| **The Last of Us Part II** | Practical environmental storytelling — decay through layered debris, fog as mood anchor, light quality for time-since-abandonment | PhotogrammetryLiteral — more action-focused pacing, decay serves traversal not contemplation | Establishes "hostile beauty" baseline — decay poetry without stalling gameplay |

Day/night cycle is constant, not cyclical | Action vocabulary for vertical traversal threat — zipline-as-organization solves level design pacing |
